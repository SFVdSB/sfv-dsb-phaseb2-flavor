# Phase B2 Initial Report: Operator Origin and Cross-Project Synergies

## Scope

This checkpoint reviews the uploaded Lorentz-violation, gauge-normalization, Landau-Zener transport, and strong-CP work against the Phase-B flavor-closure problem. It then performs the first operator-level numerical audit on the corrected regular O(4) wall.

## Symmetry clarification

The possible `U(1)` mentioned in the flavor discussion is a **candidate global flavor symmetry** (`U(1)_F`) or a discrete remnant. It is not automatically:

- electroweak hypercharge `U(1)_Y`;
- the SFV order-parameter phase symmetry;
- Peccei-Quinn `U(1)_PQ`.

The project will not break gauge hypercharge. A discrete flavor symmetry remains an equally valid option and avoids introducing an unwanted Goldstone boson.

## Synergy audit

### 1. Gauge-normalization work: directly useful methodology

The earlier gauge work introduced a local wall kernel integrated over the measured thick wall. The useful lesson is the operator architecture, not the claim that its fitted/minimal-norm Wilson coefficients were already fundamental.

For fermions, the analogous action is

\[
S_\Psi=\int d^4x\,dy\;K_A(\Phi,\phi)\,\bar\Psi_A i\Gamma^M D_M\Psi_A
-\bar\Psi_A W_A(\Phi,\phi)\Psi_A.
\]

After canonical normalization, a position-dependent `K_A` contributes a wall-normal chiral connection proportional to

\[
-\frac12\,\partial_y\ln K_A.
\]

This gives a conventional local origin for an even wall-core term without inserting an arbitrary profile by hand.

### 2. Lorentz-violation work: important guardrail

The charged-matter sector was assumed to have a standard Lorentz-invariant tree-level 4D kinetic operator, with LIV primarily in the gauge tower and only loop-suppressed percolation into charged fermions. Phase B2 therefore restricts itself to **transverse position dependence** in `K_A(y)` and `W_A(y)`. It does not add anisotropic higher-spatial-derivative operators to the brane fermion kinetic term.

### 3. Landau-Zener work: strong structural synergy

The DM/baryon work uses a local mass splitting, an avoided crossing, and an off-diagonal wall mixing. In the scalar sector, diagonalizing the local Hessian produces a rotating eigenbasis `U(theta_H(y))`. Transforming fermions into that basis creates

\[
\mathcal A_y=U^T\partial_y U=\theta_H'(y)J,
\]

which is the spatial analogue of the connection underlying an avoided crossing. This is the leading candidate explanation for why the empirically supported down-sector spacing tracks the interval between the gradient peak and maximal Hessian mixing.

The analogy is structural, not literal: flavor localization is a static zero-mode problem, whereas the DM/baryon calculation is real-time transport through a moving wall.

### 4. Strong-CP work: later phase sector and current guardrail

The strong-CP package shows that generation-dependent phase gradients readily create complex Yukawa phases. Those phases are relevant to a future CKM-CP extension, but importing them now would obscure the real-amplitude operator derivation and could create a strong-CP problem. Phase B2 therefore keeps the localization operator real. A bulk phase/PQ sector may later provide a controlled complex spurion after the amplitude hierarchy is frozen.

## Initial numerical operator audit

### Local kink term

The odd localization function can be represented by the strictly local normalized bulk-field transition

\[
T_\Phi(y)=1-2\frac{\Phi(y)-\Phi_F}{\Phi_T-\Phi_F},
\]

rather than requiring the parity-reflected construction used for numerical conditioning. Earlier equivalent-basis tests already showed that the exact flavor realization does not depend on enforcing perfect parity.

### Kinetic-normalization origin of the core term

On the central wall region, the canonical even core profile is approximated by

\[
E(y)\simeq 0.4924[-\Phi'(y)]+1.3807[-\phi'(y)].
\]

The correlation is `0.98204`. Only the shape ratio is meaningful at this stage:

\[
\frac{c_\phi}{c_\Phi}\simeq 2.804.
\]

This is exactly the profile generated by a local positive kernel of the schematic form

\[
K_A(y)=K_{0,A}\exp\{-2\eta_A[\Phi(y)+2.804\phi(y)]\},
\]

because canonical normalization contributes `-1/2 d_y ln K_A` to the chiral first-order equation. The overall strength `eta_A` remains a sector/flavor coefficient and has not been derived.

### Hessian/Berry connection

The absolute Hessian-basis connection correlates with the canonical core profile at `0.92484`. The measured landmarks are

\[
R_{\rm grad}=5.2074253,\qquad R_{\rm mix}=5.9135797,
\]

so

\[
\alpha(R_{\rm mix}-R_{\rm grad})=0.4879086,
\]

which is the Phase-B locking formula for `a_d1`. The scalar eigenbasis rotates by

\[
\Delta\theta_H=0.3489403\;\text{rad}
\]

across the same interval. This gives two distinct physical quantities:

- interval width: where the locking occurs;
- integrated connection: how much the eigenbasis rotates.

A derived flavor operator may depend on one or both.

## Crucial correction to the Phase-B interpretation

The seven post-hoc formulas are coordinates in the specific normalized `(O,E)` basis. Applying the same seven numbers unchanged after replacing that basis with strictly local operators produces large errors. Therefore the formulas themselves cannot be fundamental Wilson coefficients.

This does **not** invalidate the zero-fit numerical pattern. It changes the derivation target:

> Derive the local fermion action and its normalization first. Then calculate the effective seven controls in the canonical reporting basis.
