# Phase B2 v0.8.0 — Explicit Pati–Salam seed Lagrangian and the origin of 21

## Scope and claim boundary

The previous checkpoint found the frozen rational relations

\[
\frac{h_Q}{h_{d0}}=\frac{22}{21},\qquad
\frac{h_{u0}}{h_{d0}}=\frac{23}{21},\qquad
\frac{h_{u1}}{h_{d1}}=\frac1{21},
\]

and showed that one two-channel inverse matrix generates all three.  The missing step was to obtain

\[
\widehat M=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&22\end{pmatrix}
\]

from a local ultraviolet Lagrangian rather than inserting `21` as a matrix entry.

This checkpoint constructs such a candidate.  The integer is generated by the multiplicity of a **complete Pati–Salam adjoint seed**,

\[
\dim[\operatorname{Adj}(SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R)]
=15+3+3=21.
\]

The derivation is conditional on a universal singlet/adjoint seed threshold.  The group multiplicity is exact; the equality of the per-component threshold kernels still requires a UV seed-unification boundary condition or an induced/composite-mediator construction.

## 1. Why the elementary Casimir route is insufficient

For an elementary field in either Pati–Salam fermion representation `(4,2,1)` or `(4bar,1,2)`, the component-level gauge dressing is governed by

\[
C_2(4)+C_2(2)=\frac{15}{8}+\frac34=\frac{21}{8}.
\]

A particular charged component therefore sees `21/8`, not `21`.  Multiplying by the representation dimension `8` gives a trace equal to `21`, but that trace is not the mass correction of one component.  Consequently, a bare elementary-mediator self-energy does **not** by itself derive the required integer.

The successful construction instead uses a complete adjoint **multiplicity threshold**, for which the sum over 21 equally normalized components is physical.

## 2. Seed fields and local renormalizable Lagrangian

Introduce two heavy real scalar mediator coordinates

\[
S=(S_1,S_2),
\]

one heavy real Pati–Salam singlet seed `X_0`, and one complete real Pati–Salam adjoint seed

\[
X_{\cal A},\qquad {\cal A}=1,\ldots,21.
\]

A local renormalizable seed Lagrangian is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\rm seed}={}&
\frac12(\partial X_0)^2+
\frac12(DX_{\cal A})^2\\
&-\frac12\left[M_X^2+2\mu(S_1+S_2)\right]X_0^2\\
&-\frac12\left[M_X^2+2\mu S_2\right]X_{\cal A}X_{\cal A}
+\mathcal L_{\rm stabilizing}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The trilinear terms `mu S X^2` are dimension-four operators.  Gauge invariance is automatic because `X_A X_A` is the invariant adjoint norm and `S_1,S_2` are gauge singlets.

The field-dependent seed masses depend on `S` through two incidence vectors:

\[
v_0=(1,1),\qquad v_{\rm adj}=(0,1).
\]

The singlet seed contributes one copy of `v_0 v_0^T`.  Each of the 21 adjoint components contributes one copy of `v_adj v_adj^T`.

## 3. Quadratic threshold and exact Gram matrix

At quadratic order in `S`, a common seed threshold gives

\[
\Delta V^{(2)}
=\frac12\kappa_X S^T
\left[v_0v_0^T+
\sum_{{\cal A}=1}^{21}v_{\rm adj}v_{\rm adj}^T\right]S.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{\Delta \mathbb M_S^2}{\kappa_X}
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
+21
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
=
\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&22\end{pmatrix}}.
\]

Equivalently, define the `22 x 2` incidence matrix

\[
C=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
0&1\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

with one singlet row and 21 adjoint rows.  The mediator kernel is the pullback of the universal seed metric:

\[
C^TC=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&22\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(C^TC)=21.
\]

This is the cleanest origin of the integer.  It is a channel-counting result, not a fitted rational approximation.

### General non-universal case

Let `k_0` be the singlet threshold and `k_adj` the threshold of each adjoint component.  Factoring out `k_0`,

\[
\mathbb M_S^2/k_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&1+N_{\rm eff}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
N_{\rm eff}=21\frac{k_{\rm adj}}{k_0}.
\]

Thus exact `N=21` follows when the singlet and each adjoint component share one threshold kernel.

## 4. Coupling the mediator coordinates to the SFV wall and fermions

Use the local wall-gradient invariant already validated in Phase B2,

\[
\mathcal I_G(y)=
\frac{(\partial_y\Phi)^2+(\partial_y\phi)^2}{G_{\max}},
\]

and a wall source

\[
J_W=\mu_W\mathcal I_G(y)e_1,
\qquad e_1=(1,0).
\]

For a fermion representation `A`, introduce the Pati–Salam source vector

\[
r_A=(1,-2T_{3R}^{(A)}).
\]

The relevant values are

\[
T_{3R}(Q_L)=0,\qquad
T_{3R}(u_R)=+\frac12,\qquad
T_{3R}(d_R)=-\frac12.
\]

A local source term is schematically

\[
\mathcal L_{S\Psi}
=-\mu_W\mathcal I_G e_1^TS
-\sum_{A,i}y_A\,\bar\Psi_{Ai}\Psi_{Ai}\,r_A^TS
+\cdots.
\]

After integrating out `S`, the wall-core cross term is

\[
\Delta\mathcal L_{\rm eff}
=\mu_Wy_A\mathcal I_G(y)\bar\Psi_A\Psi_A\,
 e_1^T(\mathbb M_S^2)^{-1}r_A.
\]

For

\[
\mathbb M_S^2\propto
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&N+1\end{pmatrix},
\]

one obtains

\[
e_1^T(\mathbb M_S^2)^{-1}r_A
=\frac{N+1+2T_{3R}^{(A)}}{N}.
\]

Hence at `N=21`,

\[
d_R:1,\qquad
Q_L:\frac{22}{21},\qquad
u_R:\frac{23}{21}.
\]

The sector pattern is therefore produced by one Pati–Salam adjoint multiplicity plus the ordinary `T3R` eigenvalues.

## 7. Is threshold equality technically natural?

Pati–Salam symmetry makes all components *within each adjoint factor* degenerate.  It does not by itself force the singlet seed and all three adjoint factors to have identical masses and trilinear couplings.

A sufficient UV boundary condition is a seed-space symmetry acting on

\[
1\oplus\operatorname{Adj}(G_{PS}),
\]

which is a 22-dimensional real seed space.  An `O(22)_seed`-symmetric mass and common `S_2` coupling enforce equal seed thresholds before Pati–Salam gauging.  The `S_1` source selects only the singlet direction and supplies the rank-one deformation.

Pati–Salam gauge interactions break this seed equality radiatively.  An order marker is

\[
\left|\frac{\delta k}{k}\right|
\sim c_g\frac{g_{PS}^2\bar C_A}{16\pi^2}
\ln\frac{\Lambda}{M_X},
\qquad
\bar C_A=\frac{15(4)+3(2)+3(2)}{21}=\frac{24}{7}.
\]

For `c_g=1`, moderate couplings and a matching hierarchy of a few to ten generate percent-level splitting, comparable to the numerical tolerance.  This is encouraging but not a substitute for a complete seed-sector beta-function and threshold calculation.

An alternative is an induced/composite mediator condition: `S_1,S_2` have no independent UV quadratic kernel, and their inverse propagator is generated by the seed threshold.  In that case the Gram shape is predictive.  A generic unconstrained tree-level `S_a S_b` counterterm would otherwise erase the result.
